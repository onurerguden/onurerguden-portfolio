## Carbon numbers an auditor can check

Exporters of steel and other heavy goods to the EU now have to report the embedded emissions under the Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM). CarbonPilot AI is a five-person Google AI & Technology Academy graduation project for that work: it turns invoices, shipments and energy records into Scope 1, 2 and CBAM-focused Scope 3 emissions, retrieves the regulation behind each figure and produces a report an auditor can verify.

The rule the team set early: a language model may read documents and route work, but every emission figure comes from deterministic Python.

- opt-in LangSmith tracing;
- JSON reports with integrity hashes and downloadable PDF reports;
- the dashboard's connection to the real backend APIs.

Teammates wrote the critic's retry routing, the timeout fallback, persistence and most of the frontend.

## A graph that ends in a report or a person

The graph runs ingest, extraction, schema validation, regulation retrieval, the emission calculation, optimization and a critic review. When the critic passes, the run generates the report. When it fails, the graph retries the calculation up to the request's retry limit and then routes to a human-review node instead of returning an unchecked result.

Two outer guards sit around it. A recursion limit derived from the retry budget stops a runaway loop, and a run that takes longer than its timeout returns a marked fallback rather than a silent answer. The timeout is checked after the run finishes, so it labels a slow result; it does not cancel one.

## Keeping the model on a leash

Gemini extraction only proposes data. Its output must satisfy the same strict Pydantic schema as typed input; anything else is rejected with an explicit error, and the candidate is not saved until a person runs the calculation. Tracing is opt-in and records operational metadata only, never document text or activity data.

## Reports that show tampering

Every JSON report carries a SHA-256 hash over its canonical JSON, so a consumer can detect any change made after generation. The PDF report keeps the calculation lines and the legal references behind them.

## Planning the transition

The optimizer ranks green-transition options by net present savings under a carbon-tax schedule the user supplies. The slider simulation moves shares of energy or material use to transition levers and recomputes emissions, cost and return. The rule that matters here: legal rates, factors and costs are inputs, not hard-coded claims, and the engine rejects slider combinations that would count the same emissions twice. The 25 tests in those two modules cover these rules.

## Limitations

The extraction and optimization were tested with fixtures and a stubbed model, not on a company's real documents. Emission factors are inputs; the bundled steel data is a demo, and the default regulation references are general CBAM and GHG Protocol sources, not legal advice. The live demo is not published. Teammates' parts are described here only to place mine.
